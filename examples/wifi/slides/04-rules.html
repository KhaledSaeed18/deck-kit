Script

Three groups make the rules.

IEEE writes the technical standard. For Wi-Fi that's IEEE 802.11. New versions are called amendments, like 802.11ax.

The Wi-Fi Alliance tests devices and owns the name "Wi-Fi" and the numbers Wi-Fi 4 to 8.

In one line: 802.11 is the standard; Wi-Fi is the certification brand.

The numbers started in 2018. Wi-Fi 1 to 3 are unofficial nicknames.

3GPP does the same job for mobile networks, which is the rest of this course. It calls its versions releases (Lecture 1, slide 35).

Acronyms

If asked

  • Wi-Fi vs WLAN? WLAN is the category; Wi-Fi is the certified brand of 802.11 WLAN.
  • Who picks the numbers? The Wi-Fi Alliance, not IEEE, since October 2018: Wi-Fi 4 = 802.11n, Wi-Fi 5 = 802.11ac, Wi-Fi 6 = 802.11ax.
More detail and sources

More questions

  • Who decides the generation numbers? The Wi-Fi Alliance, not IEEE. Announced 3 October 2018: Wi-Fi 4 = 802.11n, Wi-Fi 5 = 802.11ac, Wi-Fi 6 = 802.11ax.
  • The Wi-Fi Alliance itself says "802.11 releases". Informal usage. IEEE's formal project types are amendment, corrigendum and revision; "release" is a defined term only in 3GPP.
