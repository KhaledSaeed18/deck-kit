Script

Wi-Fi from 1997 to today, one big idea per generation.

The early years. 1997, the first 802.11: 2 Mbps. In 1999, 802.11b made wireless LAN affordable at 11 Mbps, and 802.11a reached 54 Mbps on 5 GHz with a new radio technique called OFDM. In 2003, 802.11g brought the same 54 Mbps to 2.4 GHz.

People call these Wi-Fi 1, 2 and 3, but those are only nicknames.

2009, Wi-Fi 4: 600 Mbps, using several antennas at once, called MIMO.

This is the first official number. In 2018 the Wi-Fi Alliance started numbering generations at Wi-Fi 4; the older ones never got official numbers.

2013, Wi-Fi 5: about 6.9 Gbps, with wider channels.

2019, Wi-Fi 6: 9.6 Gbps, but here the goal changed from "faster" to "better when crowded". Wi-Fi 6E is the same thing on the new 6 GHz band.

2024, Wi-Fi 7: about 23 Gbps, with very wide channels and the ability to use two bands at once.

You may see 46 Gbps online; that assumed 16 antenna streams, an idea that was dropped from the final standard.

Wi-Fi 8: still being written, final expected 2028, and about the same top speed as Wi-Fi 7. Its goal is reliability instead of speed.

Overall: a factor of over 10,000 times from 1997 to today.

Acronyms

  • OFDM: Orthogonal Frequency-Division Multiplexing
  • MIMO: Multiple-Input Multiple-Output (several antennas)
  • MU-MIMO: Multi-User MIMO
  • OFDMA: OFDM Multiple Access
  • PHY: Physical layer (the raw radio rate)
  • Mbps / Gbps: megabits / gigabits per second

If asked

  • Wi-Fi 6 vs 6E? Same technology; 6E adds the 6 GHz band, subject to national regulation.
  • Why does my router box show a bigger number? Boxes add up all the bands of the router; no single device can use that total.
More detail and sources

Exact dates

For Wi-Fi 6 and 7 the year shown is when certification started. Wi-Fi 6: certified 2019, IEEE standard 2021. Wi-Fi 7: certified January 2024, IEEE approved September 2024, published July 2025.

More questions

  • Why are 802.11a and b both 1999? IEEE approved both on the same day, 16 September 1999. "Wi-Fi 1/2/3" for b/a/g is an informal convention.
  • Why is Wi-Fi 5 "5 only" when my router also has 2.4 GHz? 802.11ac is defined for 5 GHz only; the router's 2.4 GHz radio runs 802.11n (Wi-Fi 4).
  • How can certification come before the IEEE vote? The Wi-Fi Alliance certifies against a stable draft: Wi-Fi 6 certification launched in September 2019, IEEE approval came in February 2021.
