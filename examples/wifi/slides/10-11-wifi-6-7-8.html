Wi-Fi 6, 7 and 8 on one slide: from faster to smarter.

Wi-Fi 6: OFDM gave the whole channel to one user per transmission, one truck per delivery. OFDMA lets many users share one transmission: one truck carrying packages for several users. TWT (Target Wake Time) lets devices sleep until their turn. BSS coloring lets your network talk over a faint neighbour.

Wi-Fi 7 adds 320 MHz channels, 4096-QAM, and Multi-Link Operation: two bands at once, which can add speed but mainly lowers delay.

Wi-Fi 8 keeps the same peak speed and aims for Ultra High Reliability: 25% lower tail latency, 25% fewer lost frames (especially when roaming), and 25% more throughput at the same signal level.

This is the first numbered Wi-Fi generation whose top speed does not go up. The standard should be final in 2028.

Acronyms

  • OFDMA: Orthogonal Frequency-Division Multiple Access
  • TWT: Target Wake Time
  • BSS: Basic Service Set
  • QAM: Quadrature Amplitude Modulation
  • MLO: Multi-Link Operation

If asked

  • Will I get 23 Gbps? No. That is the lab maximum; a phone links at about 2.4 to 2.9 Gbps, and you measure less, about 1.5 to 2 Gbps at best.
  • Wi-Fi 6 vs 6E? Same technology; 6E adds the 6 GHz band, subject to national regulation.

Local context

Lebanon has not opened 6 GHz. TRA consulted in April 2026 on the lower part of the band, 5925 to 6425 MHz, for licence-free indoor use (the upper part, 6425 to 7125 MHz, is still undecided between Wi-Fi and mobile networks) and comments closed on 28 August 2026, with no decision as of October 2026. Here, Wi-Fi 6E and 7 run on 2.4 and 5 GHz at up to 160 MHz.
