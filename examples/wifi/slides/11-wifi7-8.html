Wi-Fi 7 and Wi-Fi 8: the plot twist

Wi-Fi 7

  • 320 MHz channels
  • 4096-QAM
  • Multi-Link Operation

Wi-Fi 8 (802.11bn)

Keeps the same peak speed. Targets Ultra High Reliability:

  • 25% lower tail latency
  • 25% fewer lost frames (especially when roaming)
  • 25% more throughput at the same signal level

Script

Now the plot twist of the talk.

Wi-Fi 7 brings 320 MHz channels, 4096-QAM, and Multi-Link Operation: use two bands at once, like driving on two roads.

Multi-Link can add speed, but its bigger win in practice is lower delay: a device uses whichever link is free right now. So the story so far: speed, then crowding, then delay.

Wi-Fi 8 keeps the same peak speed. This is the first numbered Wi-Fi generation whose top speed does not go up. Its goal is reliability.

Its targets, compared with Wi-Fi 7: 25% lower tail latency, 25% fewer lost frames (especially when roaming), and 25% more throughput at the same signal level.

In plain words: fewer slow moments, fewer dropped frames when you move between access points, and more speed at the same signal strength. Chips already appeared at CES 2026; the standard should be final in 2028.

Acronyms

  • QAM: Quadrature Amplitude Modulation
  • MLO: Multi-Link Operation
  • UHR: Ultra High Reliability (Wi-Fi 8)
  • CES: Consumer Electronics Show

If asked

  • Will I get 23 Gbps? No. That is the lab maximum: 8 antennas, the widest channel, a perfect signal. A phone with 2 antennas on a 160 MHz channel links at about 2.4 to 2.9 Gbps (an iPhone stops at 2.4), and you measure less, about 1.5 to 2 Gbps at best, because the air is shared.
  • Does a Wi-Fi 7 router speed up my old phone? Only somewhat. The standard is backward compatible, but the link runs at the client's generation. Newer clients free up airtime, which helps everyone.
  • Is Wi-Fi 8 100 Gbps? No. That came from a 2024 millimetre-wave prototype. Wi-Fi 8 keeps about 23 Gbps, like Wi-Fi 7.
More detail and sources

More questions

  • Can I use Wi-Fi 7 fully in Lebanon? Not the 6 GHz part yet. Here Wi-Fi 7 runs on 2.4 and 5 GHz, up to 160 MHz channels.
  • I read Wi-Fi 8 is 100 Gbps. That came from a 2024 millimetre-wave prototype press release. 802.11bn stays below 7.25 GHz and keeps about 23 Gbps; mmWave is a separate IEEE project (802.11bq).
  • When is Wi-Fi 8 final? Draft 2.0 passed its first working-group ballot in September 2026 (77%); IEEE predicts final approval in May 2028. Pre-standard chips (Broadcom, MediaTek) and an ASUS concept router appeared at CES 2026.

Nuances

  • Phones often use single-radio MLO (EMLSR): they listen on two links but transmit or receive on only one at a time.

Local context

Lebanon has not opened 6 GHz. TRA consulted in April 2026 on the lower part of the band, 5925 to 6425 MHz, for licence-free indoor use (the upper part, 6425 to 7125 MHz, is still undecided between Wi-Fi and mobile networks) and comments closed on 28 August 2026, with no decision as of October 2026. Here, Wi-Fi 6E and 7 run on 2.4 and 5 GHz at up to 160 MHz.
