Classifying wireless networks by coverage

Script

We group wireless networks by how far they reach. Reach decides the trade-offs: power, speed, cost, and whether you need a licence.

WPAN, Wireless Personal Area Network: a few metres around you, about 10 m. Bluetooth is the best-known example.

WLAN, Wireless Local Area Network: a building or campus, about 100 m. This is Wi-Fi, IEEE 802.11, and it's our focus today.

WMAN, Wireless Metropolitan Area Network: a city, kilometres. WiMAX was the example; it lost to 4G LTE.

WWAN, Wireless Wide Area Network: the whole country. That's cellular, 2G to 5G.

Bigger reach usually means licensed spectrum and an operator; small reach means anyone can set it up.

The boxes show reach, not "a kind of": Bluetooth is not a small Wi-Fi.

Acronyms

  • WPAN: Wireless Personal Area Network
  • WLAN: Wireless Local Area Network
  • WMAN: Wireless Metropolitan Area Network (metro = a city)
  • WWAN: Wireless Wide Area Network
  • IEEE: Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers
  • 3GPP: 3rd Generation Partnership Project
  • LTE: Long Term Evolution (4G)

If asked

  • Wi-Fi vs WLAN? WLAN is the category; Wi-Fi is the certified brand of 802.11 WLAN.
  • Is Bluetooth a kind of Wi-Fi? No. It is WPAN, a different standard and purpose, though it shares 2.4 GHz and can interfere.
  • Why did WiMAX lose? LTE had an upgrade path from GSM/UMTS and operator backing. The CDMA carriers chose LTE too, phones and chips followed LTE, and Intel stepped back in 2010.
More detail and sources

More questions

  • Is bigger coverage always licensed? No, it is a tendency. LoRaWAN is unlicensed and reaches up to 15 km rural; Wi-Fi HaLow reaches about 1 km; 5G can run in unlicensed bands (NR-U, 3GPP Release 16).
  • Is 10 m a hard limit for WPAN? No, it is the typical "personal operating space" from IEEE 802.15.1. The Bluetooth SIG says range runs from under a metre to over a kilometre.
  • Why do car keys use UWB? Distance bounding: the key is authenticated over Bluetooth LE, then UWB secure ranging measures the true distance, so a relay attack cannot fake proximity (CCC Digital Key 3.0).
  • Is WiMAX dead? The big public networks closed (Yota Moscow 2012, Sprint 2015); WiMAX-derived systems live on in niches such as airport surface links (AeroMACS).

Bluetooth history

The Bluetooth SIG has written Bluetooth since 1.0 (1999). IEEE adapted v1.1 as 802.15.1-2002/2005 and withdrew it in 2018. So it was never "originally IEEE".
