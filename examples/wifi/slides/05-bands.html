Higher frequency: faster, but shorter range

Script

Wi-Fi works in three radio bands. You often see them on your router: 2.4, 5 and 6 GHz.

2.4 GHz reaches the farthest and goes through walls best. But it's crowded: only 3 channels that don't overlap (1, 6 and 11), and microwave ovens and Bluetooth use the same band.

5 GHz is faster and less crowded, but its range is shorter.

6 GHz is the newest band: the cleanest and the fastest, used by Wi-Fi 6E, 7 and 8.

In Lebanon it is not open yet: the regulator, TRA, asked for public comments in 2026, and there is no decision so far.

The simple rule: higher frequency means more speed but shorter range.

All three bands are licence-free: no operator needed, anyone can install a router.

Acronyms

  • GHz: gigahertz, the radio frequency
  • TRA: Telecommunications Regulatory Authority (Lebanon)

If asked

  • Why is 2.4 GHz slower but reaches farther? Lower frequencies attenuate less; but the band is narrow and crowded.
  • Wi-Fi 6 vs 6E? Same technology; 6E adds the 6 GHz band, subject to national regulation.
  • Is Wi-Fi radiation harmful? Non-ionizing, low power, within regulatory limits; research inconclusive (matching Lecture 1).
More detail and sources

More questions

  • Is Wi-Fi full-duplex? No, half-duplex. Full-duplex Wi-Fi is a research topic because of self-interference (Lecture 1, slide 7: the full-duplex figure's "Self-Interference" label).
  • Do walls really block higher frequencies more? It depends on the material. In the 3GPP channel model concrete loses about 15 dB at 2.4 GHz and about 25 dB at 5 GHz, while glass and wood change by under 1 dB.
  • Where does home mesh Wi-Fi fit? Several APs forming one network, so closest to an ESS with wireless backhaul. It is not ad hoc (IBSS).

Nuances

  • 1, 6, 11 is the 11-channel plan; most of the world allows 13 channels.
  • 6 GHz range is shorter partly because the rules allow less power.

Local context

Lebanon has not opened 6 GHz. TRA consulted in April 2026 on the lower part of the band, 5925 to 6425 MHz, for licence-free indoor use (the upper part, 6425 to 7125 MHz, is still undecided between Wi-Fi and mobile networks) and comments closed on 28 August 2026, with no decision as of October 2026. Here, Wi-Fi 6E and 7 run on 2.4 and 5 GHz at up to 160 MHz.

Careful with "all unlicensed" here: the same TRA consultation shows Lebanon handles 5 GHz under a light-licence framework, at least for outdoor and higher-power use.
