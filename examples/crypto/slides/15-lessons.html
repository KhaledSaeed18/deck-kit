What chapter 1 teaches

  1. Never roll your own cipher
  2. A huge key space is not enough
  3. Only the key is secret

Script

Three things to take home.

Never design your own crypto algorithm unless a team of experienced cryptanalysts is attacking it, and never use unproven ones.

A huge key space is not enough. The substitution cipher had 2 to the 88 keys and fell to a pencil. Attackers go for the weakest link: the maths, the device or the person.

And only the key is secret. Publish the algorithm, protect the key, and use at least 128 bits for anything that must last.

Modular arithmetic, the clock maths, is the thread into the rest of the course: it is what public-key cryptography runs on.
