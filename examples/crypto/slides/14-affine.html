Script

One upgrade: multiply by a, then add b. The key is the pair a, b.

To decrypt we must divide by a, which needs an inverse mod 26, which needs gcd of a and 26 to be 1. That leaves 12 possible values of a: the odd numbers except 13.

So the key space is 12 times 26, 312 keys. A laptop tries them all in a fraction of a second. And the mapping is still fixed letter to letter, so frequency analysis works as before.

With key 9, 13, ATTACK becomes NCCNFZ. Notice the double C: the T T leaks straight through.

So what do we take away from chapter 1?

If asked

  • How do you decrypt? x = a-1 · (y minus b) mod 26. For a = 9 the inverse is 3, because 9 · 3 = 27 = 1 mod 26.
  • Does encrypting twice help? No: two affine ciphers combine into one affine cipher, so the key space does not grow (problem 1.14).
