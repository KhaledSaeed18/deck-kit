Alice, Bob and Oscar

Script

Every crypto story has the same cast. Alice wants to send Bob a message.

She feeds the plaintext x into an encryption function e and gets ciphertext y. It crosses an insecure channel, which could be the Internet or the air between your phone and a Wi-Fi router. Bob decrypts with d and gets x back.

Oscar, named to remind you of "opponent", can read everything on that channel. Listening like this is called eavesdropping. With a good cipher, y looks like random bits to him.

The catch: Alice and Bob need the same key k, and it must travel over a secure channel, even a person carrying it. So the problem of sending a message secretly becomes the problem of sending and storing a key secretly.

Next

And here is the counterintuitive part: the algorithm is public.

If asked

  • What are x, y, k called? Plaintext or cleartext, ciphertext, and key. All possible keys form the key space.
  • Does encryption also stop Oscar changing the message? Not on its own. This picture is only about confidentiality; integrity and sender authentication come later in the book.
