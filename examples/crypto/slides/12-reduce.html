Reduce early, keep numbers small

The long way

38 = 6561

6561 ≡ 2 mod 7

The smart way

38 = 81 · 81

≡ 4 · 4 = 16 ≡ 2 mod 7

Script

Because any member of a class works, we can swap big numbers for small ones mid-calculation. Let's compute 3 to the 8 mod 7.

The long way: 3 to the 8 is 6561, and dividing that by 7 in your head is not fun. The answer is 2.

The smart way: 3 to the 8 is 81 times 81. 81 is 4 mod 7, so this is 4 times 4, 16, which is 2. Same answer, and no number bigger than 81.

This is not a party trick. RSA raises 2048-bit numbers to 2048-bit powers. Without reducing early the intermediate results would not fit in the universe.

If asked

  • Can we always divide mod m? Only by numbers that have an inverse, which happens exactly when gcd(a, m) = 1. 15 has an inverse mod 26 (it is 7); 14 does not, since gcd(14, 26) = 2.
