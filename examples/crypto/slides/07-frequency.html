The letters give it away

WE WILL MEET IN THE MIDDLE OF THE LIBRARY AT NOON

Script

Here is the weakness. The same plaintext letter always becomes the same ciphertext letter, so the statistics of the language survive encryption.

In English, E is about 13 percent of all letters, then T, then A.

Now count the ciphertext from the start of the talk. One letter, q, clearly stands out.

So q is almost certainly E. Add letter pairs, like Q almost always followed by U, and short words like THE and AND, and the table falls apart one letter at a time.

And there it is: "We will meet in the middle of the library at noon. All arrangements are made." No key search at all.

The lesson: a large key space is necessary but not enough. A good cipher must hide the statistics of the plaintext.

If asked

  • Does this work on short texts? Surprisingly well. Even short texts are close to the language's distribution, and pairs and short words fill the gaps. This text has only 61 letters.
  • Why is h second here, not T? Short texts wander. h is A; the next three are tied at five each, so you never rely on exact ranks, only on the overall shape.
