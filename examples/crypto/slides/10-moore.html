Moore's law is compound interest

Script

Key lengths are a bet on the future. The book uses Moore's law as a rough guide: computing power doubles every 18 months at the same cost.

Say breaking a cipher today takes one month and a million dollars of hardware. In 18 months it costs half a million. In three years, a quarter.

After 15 years that is ten doublings, 2 to the 10, about a thousand times cheaper. The same attack costs about a thousand dollars, or a million dollars buys it in about 43 minutes. Exponential growth is a bank account paying 50 percent interest.

That is why long-term secrets need keys with a big margin.

If asked

  • Is Moore's law still true? Moore's own 1975 figure was every two years; 18 months is the popular version. Treat it as an illustration, not a forecast.
  • The book says 45 minutes? One month divided by 1024 is about 42 to 44 minutes, so "about 43" is closer.
