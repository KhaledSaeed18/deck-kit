Two sides of one field

Script

The umbrella word is cryptology, not cryptography.

It has two sides. Cryptography makes ciphers. Cryptanalysis breaks them. Breaking is not a side business for criminals: it is the only way we ever learn whether a cipher is actually secure.

Cryptography has three branches. Symmetric: both sides share one secret key, which is all cryptography until 1976. Asymmetric, or public-key: each user also has a public key. And protocols, which combine them into things like TLS in your browser.

Real systems mix them, which the book calls hybrid schemes.

If asked

  • Where do hash functions go? The book treats them as a third class of algorithm that shares properties with symmetric ciphers (chapter 11).
  • Was 1976 really the start of public-key cryptography? Publicly, yes: Diffie, Hellman and Merkle. GCHQ found the same ideas in secret between 1970 and 1974; that was declassified in 1997.
