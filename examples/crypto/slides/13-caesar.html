Caesar's shift

e(x) ≡ x + k mod 26

d(y) ≡ y − k mod 26

Only 26 keys. Try them all over coffee.

Script

Caesar's cipher is the clock with 26 positions. Number the letters A as 0 up to Z as 25, and turn the inner ring by three: A becomes d, B becomes e, and X, Y, Z wrap round to a, b, c.

As maths: encrypt by adding the key mod 26, decrypt by subtracting it. With key 17, ATTACK becomes RKKRTB.

And it is hopeless as security. There are only 26 keys, so you just try them all and read which output is English. Letter frequency breaks it too, since it is a special case of substitution.

What does all this teach us?

If asked

  • Did Caesar really use three? According to Suetonius, yes: D for A. The book says "allegedly".
  • Why is the key only 0 to 25? A shift of 27 is the same as a shift of 1; the key lives in Z26.
