Arithmetic on a clock

8 + 4 = 12 ≡ 3 mod 9

42 = 4 · 9 + 6

42 ≡ 6 mod 9

keep only the remainder

Script

Every cipher in this course does arithmetic in a finite set. You already know one: a clock. Keep adding hours and you never leave 1 to 12.

Here is a clock with nine positions, 0 to 8.

8 plus 4 is 12, but on this dial you walk four steps from 8, wrap past 0, and land on 3. We write 8 plus 4 is congruent to 3 mod 9.

The rule: divide by the modulus and keep the remainder. 42 is 4 times 9 plus 6, so 42 is congruent to 6 mod 9.

Formally, a is congruent to r mod m when m divides a minus r. That has a surprise: the remainder is not unique. 12, 3, 21 and minus 6 are all the same thing mod 9. They form one equivalence class.

So in practice we reduce: keep only the remainder, here 0 to 8.

If asked

  • Which remainder do we write down? By convention the one from 0 to m minus 1, but any member of the class gives the same final answer.
  • What is Zm? The integer ring: the numbers 0 to m minus 1 with addition and multiplication mod m.
